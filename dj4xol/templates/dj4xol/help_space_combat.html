Space Combat

Space combat is resolved by location. If two or more players have fleets at the same coordinates, each side contributes a combat strength based on ships, integrity, race traits, and fleet combat levels.

How Combat Is Triggered

Space combat is automatic. You do not press a separate attack button. Combat triggers during turn processing whenever fleets from at least two different players occupy the same map coordinates after movement is resolved.

To initiate combat, plan movement so your fleet arrives at an occupied location (or stays in place while another player moves in). Intercept and patrol orders can also create contact by steering fleets into enemy positions.

To prepare for combat, players typically: build and commission newer fleets after key research unlocks, keep fleet integrity high before contact, and combine multiple fleets at likely engagement points.

How Fleet Strength Is Built

A fleet's strength starts from its ship count with diminishing returns. Larger fleets are stronger, but each additional ship adds less than the previous one.

That base is then scaled by offensive power and reduced by the opponent's defensive power. Offensive and defensive power come from race multipliers and the fleet's stored combat levels. These stored levels are applied when the fleet is commissioned.

Commissioning happens when a new fleet is produced at a colony with a shipyard. At that moment, your unlocked technologies are evaluated and converted into the fleet's permanent combat profile. The game takes the latest unlocked technology in each relevant technology type and combines the resulting offense and defence effects into the new fleet.

This means two fleets built by the same empire at different times can have different stored combat levels if research progressed between build years. Existing fleets keep their stored combat levels until they are lost or merged; new breakthroughs mainly affect newly commissioned fleets.

Fleet integrity then applies a survivability curve. A fully healthy fleet contributes full strength, while damaged fleets lose a disproportionately large share of effective power.

How Sides Are Compared

At each combat location, fleets are grouped by player. The side's total strength is the sum of its fleets. Enemy defence is weighted by enemy ship counts, so large enemy fleets influence the defensive profile more.

The winner is selected with weighted probability from side strengths. Luck does not replace strength, but it adds a modest random jitter to each side's effective weight before the winner roll.

Damage Resolution

A fixed damage pool is divided between sides according to relative enemy strength. The side facing stronger opposition takes more total damage.

Each side's damage is then distributed to its fleets by ship-count share. Fleets can lose integrity, lose ships when badly damaged, and be destroyed. Combat can also generate salvage when hulls are destroyed or heavily damaged.

Simple Example

If two sides are equal, each usually takes about half of the total damage pool. If one side is much stronger, that side tends to take much less damage, but it can still suffer losses, especially if luck and integrity effects move against it.
