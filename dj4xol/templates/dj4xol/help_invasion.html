Invasion

Invasion occurs when a fleet unloads colonists onto an enemy-owned colony. Resolution happens in two stages: planetary defence fire first, then ground combat if invaders remain.

How Invasion Is Triggered

Invasion is initiated through fleet orders. Move a fleet carrying colonists to an enemy-owned star, then issue a transfer order that unloads colonists to that star. During turn processing, unloading colonists onto an enemy colony automatically triggers invasion resolution for that transfer.

You can plan invasions by combining movement and transfer orders: first ensure the fleet reaches the target system, then unload the intended number of colonists. Larger invasion forces are stronger on the ground, but fleet survival still depends on planetary defence fire in Stage 1.

Defenders can prepare by maintaining staffed defences, increasing population, and improving colony-defence technology, all of which raise resistance to invasion.

Stage 1: Planetary Defence Fire

Colony defences are converted to an effective defence force before firing. Employment is applied first: if your colony does not have enough population to staff all infrastructure jobs, effective defences are reduced below the raw defence count.

The colony's defensive multiplier is then applied. This combines race defence traits with the defender's latest unlocked colony-defence technology.

The invading fleet's strength is evaluated against that defence profile using the same core combat arithmetic as space engagements. This can damage or destroy the invading fleet before any troops reach the surface.

Stage 2: Ground Combat

If the fleet survives, troops and defenders fight on the ground. Invaders are the unloaded colonists converted to population units. Defenders are the colony's current population.

Each side is scaled by race ground-force multipliers. The side with the larger ground force wins. Remaining population is calculated from the surviving force after subtracting the opponent's force.

Ownership and Losses

If invaders win, colony ownership changes immediately and surviving invaders become the new population. If defenders hold, ownership remains unchanged and surviving defenders stay on the world.

In all cases, the unloaded colonists are removed from the invading fleet. Combat reports are sent to both sides describing losses and fleet damage.

Practical Notes

A high raw defence count is strongest when properly staffed. Understaffing can dramatically reduce effective defences, while colony-defence technology can significantly multiply them.

Defences are unusual among infrastructure in that they are not penalised by overstaffing. The major labour risk is understaffing.
