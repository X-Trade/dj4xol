Mining, Salvage & Asteroids

Surface Minerals

Stars and salvage can already have minerals sitting on the surface. A fleet at the location can load them directly with a Transfer order.

Remote Mining

Remote mining is different from surface transfer. You send a fleet to a star and give it a Remote Mine order. That fleet then pulls minerals up from underground deposits over time until its hold is full or the order changes.

Why Colonies Still Want Mines

A proper colony with mines is the long-term way to keep extracting local mineral wealth. If you keep mining at a moderate level rather than stripping the world as fast as possible, a colony can continue to draw useful minerals for a very long time. Remote mining is mainly an alternative for depleted colonies, awkward worlds you do not want to settle properly, or late-game optimisation.

Salvage Types

Salvage is the general wreckage left behind by destroyed fleets. Most of it is straightforward to recover, but fresh combat salvage can sometimes be unstable.
Asteroid fields are mineral-rich salvage sites that can be looted by transfer, but working them is not always completely risk-free.
Ancient debris is another recoverable site type, and it is best treated with more caution than ordinary salvage.

Using Asteroid Fields

Move a fleet onto the asteroid field and use Transfer to load the minerals you want. This is direct pickup, not remote mining.

Plain salvage from combat may occasionally inflict light damage when a fleet lingers on it. Asteroid fields are a little more hazardous than ordinary salvage, while jettisoned cargo dumped into space is safe to recover.

Practical Early Use

If a new colony is short on ironium, a nearby salvage or asteroid site can be a fast way to bootstrap mines and factories.
