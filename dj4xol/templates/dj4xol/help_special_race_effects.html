Special Race Effects

This page covers two strategy-critical race flags: Parasitic and Mechanical. It focuses on how they play on the map and what that means for your decisions each turn.

Parasitic (is_parasitic)

Parasitic races are uniquely suited to hostile colonist raids. If you load colonists from a non-allied enemy colony, the encounter messaging uses abduction wording and your raid faces lighter resistance than normal races.

For non-parasitic races, hostile colonist raids are much harsher: high pickup losses and guaranteed integrity damage are expected outcomes.

Parasitic defenders also have a special invasion interaction. If another race invades and fails, the invader colonist losses are absorbed into the parasitic colony population instead of being extinguished.

Allied transfers are treated as normal logistics, not raids. In practice, this means abduction is a wartime tool, while alliances remain the safer route for deliberate population movement.

Mechanical (is_mechanical)

Mechanical races do not rely on natural population growth the same way organic races do. Instead, they get dedicated production orders to manufacture colonists directly.

  • Produce 1k Colonists: low mineral cost, uses existing colonists as workforce.
  • Produce 1m Colonists: high mineral cost, also requires existing colonists.

These production orders only appear for mechanical races. If you are mechanical, your growth planning should treat colonist production as a core economy task, not an optional late-game extra.

Practical Strategy

  • Parasitic: keep fast raid fleets active and look for vulnerable non-allied colonies.
  • Parasitic: use diplomacy intentionally, because allied states disable raid pressure.
  • Mechanical: reserve minerals and production slots for colonist manufacturing, especially when employment is tight.
  • Mechanical: avoid assuming natural growth will bail out a stalled colony plan.
